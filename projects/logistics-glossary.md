# Logistics Glossary — HA R6 Project

**Purpose:** A plain-English reference for logistics, freight, customs, Amazon inbound, and 3PL terms used in the R6 AGL + AWD cost comparison.

**Use this when:** Reading a freight quote, speaking with a forwarder or 3PL, comparing routes, or clarifying what a charge or responsibility means.

> **Important:** A term alone does not tell us what a provider's quote includes. Always confirm the named place, service scope, payer, included charges, exclusions, and applicable Incoterm in writing.

## 1. Incoterms and shipping responsibility

Incoterms are internationally recognized trade terms that allocate delivery responsibilities, costs, and risk between seller and buyer. They do not, by themselves, set the product price, payment terms, ownership/title transfer, or every detail of the sale contract. Use the exact Incoterm version and named place/port (for example, **FOB Yantian, Incoterms® 2020**).

| Term | Plain-English meaning | What it usually means for HA |
|---|---|---|
| **EXW — Ex Works** | Seller makes goods available at its premises or another named place. The buyer generally arranges pickup, export clearance, and the rest of the journey. | HA/its forwarder may need to manage pickup and export formalities. Confirm who can legally handle export clearance in China. |
| **FCA — Free Carrier** | Seller delivers goods, export-cleared, to the carrier or other person nominated by the buyer at the named place. | Often useful for containerized shipments; specify the exact handover point. |
| **FOB — Free On Board** | Seller delivers the goods on board the vessel nominated by the buyer at the named port of shipment and clears them for export. Risk transfers when the goods are on board. Buyer arranges/pays the main ocean freight and subsequent transport. | With **FOB Yantian**, for example, the supplier's responsibility generally runs through export clearance and loading on board at Yantian. HA/its forwarder generally controls the ocean booking and onward costs. Confirm origin terminal/handling charges and any supplier-side charges separately. |
| **CFR — Cost and Freight** | Seller arranges and pays ocean freight to the named destination port, but risk transfers to the buyer once goods are on board at origin. | Freight may be included in the supplier's price, but risk does not stay with the seller until arrival. Insurance is not included by this term. |
| **CIF — Cost, Insurance and Freight** | Like CFR, but seller also obtains the required minimum cargo insurance for the named destination port. Risk still transfers on board at origin. | Do not assume CIF means the seller bears transit risk until arrival; check the insurance coverage and destination charges. |
| **CPT — Carriage Paid To** | Seller pays carriage to the named destination, while risk transfers when goods are handed to the first carrier. | Can apply to multimodal transport. Confirm the handover point and what destination services are excluded. |
| **CIP — Carriage and Insurance Paid To** | Like CPT, but seller also obtains specified cargo insurance. Risk transfers at handover to the first carrier. | Check the actual insurance certificate, coverage, and exclusions. |
| **DAP — Delivered at Place** | Seller arranges transport to the named destination and makes goods available for unloading. Buyer normally handles import clearance, duties, and taxes. | Confirm who pays destination handling and final delivery, and whether Amazon/3PL can receive the delivery. |
| **DPU — Delivered at Place Unloaded** | Seller delivers and unloads goods at the named destination. Buyer handles import clearance and duties/taxes. | Confirm what unloading means at the actual facility and whether special equipment or appointments cost extra. |
| **DDP — Delivered Duty Paid** | Seller arranges delivery and handles import clearance and applicable duties/taxes to the named destination. | Convenient in principle, but confirm who is importer of record, how duties/taxes are calculated, and whether the provider can legally perform the import process. |

### FOB — the practical answer

**FOB means the supplier is responsible for getting the goods export-cleared and loaded on board the vessel at the named port. The buyer generally arranges and pays the ocean freight from there, plus insurance if desired, import clearance, duties/taxes, destination handling, and onward delivery. Risk transfers when the goods are on board the vessel at origin.**

FOB is intended for sea and inland-waterway transport. For containerized cargo handed to a carrier at a terminal before it is loaded on the vessel, **FCA may be more appropriate**. Do not change the agreed Incoterm casually; ask the supplier/forwarder to confirm the correct handover point.

**When someone asks whether you understand FOB, the operational follow-up is:** “Yes. Can you confirm the named port and whether the quote includes origin pickup, export clearance, terminal/handling charges, ocean freight, destination charges, customs clearance, duties, and final delivery?”

## 2. Freight providers and transport

| Term | Plain-English meaning |
|---|---|
| **Freight forwarder** | A company that arranges transportation and related logistics with carriers and service providers. It may coordinate ocean/air freight, customs brokerage, trucking, and documentation. A forwarder is not automatically the vessel operator or customs broker. |
| **Carrier** | The company that physically transports the cargo, such as an ocean shipping line, airline, rail operator, or trucking company. |
| **AGL — Amazon Global Logistics** | Amazon's freight service for moving eligible inventory internationally into Amazon's logistics network. Confirm the exact service scope and current rate/benefit terms for each shipment. |
| **Ocean freight** | The charge for moving cargo by sea between origin and destination ports. It may be quoted per container or by volume/weight for less-than-container-load cargo. |
| **FCL — Full Container Load** | A shipment booked as a full container, such as a 20-foot or 40-foot container. The container may not be physically full; the pricing is for the container. |
| **LCL — Less than Container Load** | Cargo sharing container space with other shippers' cargo. Charges are often based on volume or chargeable weight, with minimums and handling fees. |
| **Air freight** | Cargo transported by aircraft, usually faster and more expensive per unit than ocean freight. |
| **Drayage** | Short-distance trucking, commonly moving a container between a port, rail terminal, warehouse, or nearby facility. |
| **Final-mile delivery** | The last transport leg to the destination, such as a 3PL or Amazon fulfillment center. |
| **Transload** | Moving cargo from one transport unit/container to another, often from an ocean container into a domestic truck or trailer. |
| **Intermodal** | Using multiple transport modes (for example, ocean, rail, and truck) within one shipment. |
| **FTL — Full Truckload** | A truck shipment booked for the use of the truck or trailer. |
| **LTL — Less than Truckload** | Freight sharing truck space with other shipments. It may involve terminals and additional handling. |
| **CBM — Cubic Meter** | A volume measurement used in freight pricing. Calculate as length × width × height in meters, summed across packages. |
| **Chargeable weight** | The weight used to price freight, often the greater of actual gross weight and dimensional/volumetric weight under the provider's rules. |
| **Transit time** | Estimated time cargo spends moving between stated points. It is not necessarily the full door-to-door lead time. |
| **Lead time** | Total elapsed time across preparation, pickup, export, transit, customs, delivery, and receiving. Ask providers which stages their estimate covers. |
| **ETD / ETA** | Estimated Time of Departure / Estimated Time of Arrival. These are estimates and can change. |
| **Demurrage** | A charge for keeping a carrier's container at a port/terminal beyond the allowed free time. |
| **Detention** | A charge for keeping a carrier's container outside the terminal beyond the allowed free time before returning it. Provider definitions and local tariff wording can vary. |
| **Free time** | The allowed period before demurrage or detention charges begin. Confirm the number of days and when the clock starts/stops. |

## 3. Customs, import, and shipping documents

| Term | Plain-English meaning |
|---|---|
| **Customs clearance** | The process of declaring goods to customs and satisfying import/export requirements so cargo can legally move. |
| **Customs broker** | A licensed/authorized professional or company that handles customs declarations and clearance on behalf of an importer/exporter. |
| **Importer of Record (IOR)** | The party legally responsible for ensuring the import entry is accurate and that applicable duties, taxes, and regulatory requirements are met. Confirm the IOR before booking. |
| **Duty / tariff** | Government charge on imported goods, generally determined by classification, origin, customs value, and applicable trade rules. |
| **HTS / HS code** | Harmonized tariff classification used to identify goods for customs and determine applicable duty and regulatory treatment. Confirm classification with a qualified broker; do not guess. |
| **Customs value** | The value used by customs to assess duties and other import requirements. It may not be identical to the commercial invoice total in every situation. |
| **ISF — Importer Security Filing** | A U.S. advance cargo data filing generally required for ocean imports, commonly called “10+2.” Confirm filing responsibility and deadlines with the broker/forwarder. |
| **Commercial invoice** | Seller's document describing the sale, goods, values, parties, and trade terms; used for customs and shipment processing. |
| **Packing list** | Document showing package count, contents, dimensions, and weights. |
| **Bill of Lading (B/L or BL)** | A transport document issued for ocean cargo. It can serve as a receipt, evidence of the contract of carriage, and, depending on its form, a document of title. |
| **Air Waybill (AWB)** | Air cargo transport document. It is generally not a negotiable document of title. |
| **Proof of Delivery (POD)** | Evidence that a shipment was delivered, usually with date, recipient, and delivery details. |
| **Cargo insurance** | Coverage against specified loss or damage during transit. Confirm insured value, covered risks, deductible, exclusions, and claims process. |
| **Origin charges** | Costs incurred at or near origin, such as pickup, export customs, documentation, consolidation, and origin terminal handling. The exact inclusions vary by quote. |
| **Destination charges** | Costs incurred at or near destination, such as terminal handling, documentation, customs-related services, and release fees. The exact inclusions vary by quote. |
| **Accessorial charges** | Additional service charges outside the base transportation rate, such as liftgate, appointment, residential delivery, sorting, or special handling. |

## 4. 3PL and warehouse operations

| Term | Plain-English meaning |
|---|---|
| **3PL — Third-Party Logistics** | An outside logistics provider that may receive, store, handle, prepare, and ship inventory for a business. |
| **Receiving** | Checking inbound goods into a warehouse, including counts, condition checks, and system receipt. Ask whether pricing is per shipment, pallet, carton, unit, or labor hour. |
| **Unloading** | Removing cargo from a container or truck. Confirm whether the quote includes floor-loaded container unloading, palletized unloading, labor, and equipment. |
| **Floor-loaded** | Cartons loaded directly onto the container floor rather than stacked on pallets. Usually requires more unloading labor. |
| **Palletization** | Placing cartons onto pallets and securing them for storage or transport. Ask whether pallets, labor, wrapping, and materials are included. |
| **Pallet** | A platform used to stack and move goods. Confirm pallet dimensions, height, weight, and any destination-specific requirements. |
| **Storage fee** | Charge for keeping inventory at a warehouse, often per pallet, cubic foot, bin, or day/month. Check minimums and billing rules. |
| **Handling fee** | Charge for warehouse labor or movement, such as receiving, picking, sorting, labeling, or preparing an outbound shipment. Ask exactly which activity it covers. |
| **Pick and pack** | Selecting inventory and preparing it for an outbound order or shipment. |
| **Outbound freight** | Cost to move inventory from the 3PL to the next destination, such as Amazon FBA. |
| **Cross-docking** | Receiving goods and quickly transferring them to outbound transport with little or no storage. |
| **Inventory count / cycle count** | Checking physical stock against warehouse records, either periodically or continuously. |
| **Warehouse appointment** | A scheduled delivery/receiving slot. Missed or late appointments can cause delays or fees. |
| **Pallet exchange** | Swapping pallets at delivery. Confirm whether the facility requires a specific pallet type or charges for non-exchange. |

## 5. Amazon programs and inbound terms

| Term | Plain-English meaning |
|---|---|
| **FBA — Fulfillment by Amazon** | Amazon stores eligible seller inventory in its fulfillment network and handles customer order fulfillment and related services. |
| **AWD — Amazon Warehousing and Distribution** | Amazon's bulk storage and distribution service that can hold inventory and replenish eligible FBA inventory. Check current program fees, eligibility, and service terms. |
| **AWD replenishment** | Movement of inventory from AWD into FBA based on the applicable replenishment process/settings. Confirm the actual charges and timing for HA's account. |
| **Inbound shipment** | Inventory being sent into an Amazon fulfillment or storage network. |
| **Shipment plan / Send to Amazon** | Seller Central workflow used to provide shipment details and arrange eligible inbound inventory deliveries. |
| **Inbound placement** | Amazon's process for assigning or offering destinations for inbound inventory. Available options and fees depend on marketplace, shipment, and current policy. |
| **Placement fee** | A fee that may apply depending on the inbound placement option and current Amazon rules. Do not assume a fee waiver; verify it in the actual shipment workflow or current fee schedule. |
| **Receiving** | Amazon's process of checking in and making delivered inventory available in its network. Delivery does not mean inventory is immediately received or sellable. |
| **FC — Fulfillment Center** | An Amazon facility that stores and fulfills customer orders. |
| **FNSKU** | Amazon barcode identifier used to identify a seller's specific inventory for FBA when required. |
| **Carton content information** | Details identifying the products and quantities in each carton, used to support inbound receiving. |
| **SPD — Small Parcel Delivery** | Sending cartons individually through parcel carriers rather than palletized freight. |
| **LTL / FTL inbound** | Sending palletized freight to Amazon using less-than-truckload or full-truckload transportation, subject to current marketplace requirements. |
| **Appointment delivery** | A scheduled freight delivery to a receiving facility. Confirm who books it and whether the quote includes appointment coordination. |
| **Stranded inventory** | Amazon inventory that is in a fulfillment center but is not available for sale due to a listing or other issue. |
| **Unfulfillable inventory** | Inventory Amazon has classified as unable to fulfill customer orders. It may require removal, disposal, or other action. |

## 6. Costs and comparison language

| Term | Plain-English meaning |
|---|---|
| **Base rate** | The starting price for a service before surcharges, accessorials, taxes, and other fees. |
| **Surcharge** | An additional charge on top of the base rate, such as fuel, peak-season, or security surcharge. |
| **All-in quote** | A quote intended to include all specified services and charges for a defined route. Never assume “all-in” means every possible cost; request an itemized inclusion/exclusion list. |
| **Landed cost** | Total cost to get inventory to the defined destination, including applicable product, freight, customs, duty, and handling costs. State exactly where the destination point is. |
| **Total cost of ownership (TCO)** | The full cost of a route or operating model over the relevant period, including direct fees and material ongoing costs. |
| **Cost per unit** | Total comparable cost divided by the number of units in the shipment. Use the same unit basis across options. |
| **Cost per CBM** | Cost divided by shipment volume in cubic meters. Useful when a quote is volume-based. |
| **Quote validity** | Period during which the quoted rate is available. Freight rates may change after expiry or due to capacity/fuel changes. |
| **Minimum charge** | Lowest amount a provider bills even if calculated volume, weight, or activity would produce a lower charge. |
| **Variable vs. fixed cost** | Variable costs change with shipment size or activity; fixed costs do not change directly with volume over the relevant range. |
| **Confirmed / estimated / unknown** | Label every comparison input according to evidence. A quote is not confirmed if it excludes key route costs or depends on unverified assumptions. |

## 7. R6 route shorthand

| Route | Flow | Main cost components to verify |
|---|---|---|
| **Current baseline** | China supplier → AGL → AWD → FBA | AGL freight and included services; AWD receiving/storage/handling/replenishment fees; applicable Amazon placement benefits or waivers; any other account-specific charges. |
| **Direct-to-FBA** | China supplier → independent forwarder → FBA | Supplier/origin charges; export clearance; ocean freight; insurance; customs brokerage; duties/taxes; destination handling; drayage/final-mile; Amazon inbound/placement fees; appointment and compliance costs. |
| **Via 3PL** | China supplier → independent forwarder → 3PL → FBA | All applicable direct-route international costs plus 3PL receiving/unloading, palletization, storage, handling, labeling/prep, outbound freight, and Amazon inbound/placement fees. |

### Minimum questions to ask every provider

1. What exact route and named origin/destination does this quote cover?
2. Which Incoterm does the price assume (for example, FOB Yantian, Incoterms® 2020)?
3. What services and charges are included?
4. What charges are excluded or billed separately at origin and destination?
5. Does it include export clearance, customs brokerage, duties/taxes, insurance, port/terminal fees, drayage, and final delivery? If not, who arranges and pays each?
6. Is the rate for FCL or LCL, and what container/volume/weight assumptions apply?
7. What are the estimated transit time and full door-to-door lead time?
8. What free time, demurrage, detention, minimum charges, and surcharges may apply?
9. Is delivery to the named Amazon FC or 3PL included, including appointment requirements?
10. How long is the quote valid, and what could change the final invoice?

## 8. R6 comparison guardrails

- Compare the same shipment origin, destination, quantity, carton/pallet count, dimensions, weight, and service level.
- Compare **total cost to the same endpoint** (for example, inventory delivered to FBA), not ocean freight alone.
- Separate one-time shipment charges from recurring storage and handling charges.
- Include relevant AGL/AWD program costs and benefits only when verified for the shipment and account.
- Do not assume a 3PL is cheaper until receiving, storage, handling, outbound freight, and any extra Amazon fees are included.
- Mark every input as **Quoted**, **Verified from HA/Amazon records**, **Estimated**, or **Unknown**. Record the source and date.
- Treat all alternative routes as cost scenarios until HA explicitly approves a routing change. This glossary does not authorize a booking or operational change.

---

**Reference note:** Incoterm explanations follow the general structure of Incoterms® 2020. For a live shipment, confirm the exact term, named place/port, contract wording, customs obligations, and current carrier/Amazon requirements with the supplier, forwarder, broker, and relevant official guidance.
